# Positioning and Offers

Read [shared policy](references/core-policy.md) first. Use [sources](references/sources.md) for current-source checks and boundaries.

1. Map the customer journey from problem discovery to repeat purchase. Choose a single campaign objective: qualified lead, booked appointment, purchase, renewal or another defined conversion. Define what counts and what is excluded.
2. Read actual pricing, costs, capacity, proof and differentiation. State approved benefits and unsupported claims separately. For lead businesses estimate maximum acquisition cost from verified close rate and contribution margin, labeling assumptions. Do not optimize for cheap forms if downstream sales are poor.
3. Compare rival offers and public landing pages. Find underserved objections, convenience gaps, trust gaps and audience segments. Propose an original differentiator rather than matching every competitor feature.
4. Build a 30-day experiment backlog, prioritizing expected mechanism, evidence, impact, effort, cost and reversibility. Start with small independently testable changes: hook, offer, format or CTA. Preserve a control and avoid confounding simultaneous funnel changes.
5. For each experiment define target audience, exclusion, channel, creative, landing destination, primary KPI, guardrails, sample/data needs, stop/review criteria and owner. Determine budget from available authority; do not invent a safe universal spend amount or guaranteed return.
6. Include an organic distribution alternative and a lead follow-up plan. Do not describe free reach as free labor or free external tooling. Record acquisition cost separately from media spend.

Return a positioning statement, evidence-backed offer, prioritized test plan and explicit unknowns. Reuse existing business decisions unless evidence or user steering changes them.
