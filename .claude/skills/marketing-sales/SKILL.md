# Follow-up and Sales Conversion

Read [shared policy](references/core-policy.md) first. Use [sources](references/sources.md) for current-source checks and boundaries.

1. Model real stages: new → validated → qualified → contacted → booked → attended → proposal → won/lost. Adapt to the actual business instead of forcing a B2B sequence onto retail. Define who owns each stage and its exit evidence.
2. For opted-in inbound leads, draft a useful acknowledgment, answer common objections, provide the requested next step and offer a booking path. Use the existing authorized SMTP/provider and existing account identities. Do not bulk-send through an unverified personal Gmail setup.
3. Establish sequence timing, local timezones, channel permissions, recipient scope, frequency ceiling, quiet hours and stop conditions. Stop on opt-out, wrong person, complaint, disqualification, booking/win or reply requiring human attention. Suppression is checked immediately before every send, including retries.
4. Personalize using verified relevant facts. Do not imply a human relationship that does not exist, fabricate a prior conversation or guarantee outcomes. Drafts remain drafts until actual sending is authorized. Record message IDs, actor, authorization, delivery/bounce/reply and status without logging credentials.
5. Measure response time, qualification rate, booked-to-attended, lead-to-sale, sales cycle and contribution. Track why leads were rejected or lost and feed those objections into new creative and funnel experiments.
6. For repeat purchases, propose useful education, replenishment reminders, referral invitations and reactivation only within permitted consent and suppression rules. Health-related communications need appropriate privacy and advertising review.

Return the next best action per legitimate lead, draft sequence and pipeline summary. Distinguish sent, delivered, replied, qualified and sold. Never count an AI lead score as a sale.
